# Proposal: a companion paper on the formal proof of the Kurzweil–Netter theorem

This document is a proposal artifact, not a draft: an annotated outline of a possible companion paper to *SmallLatticeReps*, together with a map from each proposed section to the source material that already exists.  It is meant to support a discussion among the *SmallLatticeReps* coauthors about whether to write the paper, how to divide content between the two papers, and who its authors should be.  Nothing beyond this outline has been written.

## The pitch

The theorem (representable finite lattices are closed under dualization) was proved by Kurzweil on the group side (1985) and generalized by Netter to congruence lattices of finite algebras (1986), but Netter's article apparently never appeared, so no published proof exists.  *SmallLatticeReps* plans to give the first published informal proof.  Meanwhile the agda-algebras library now contains what we believe is the first machine-checked proof of the theorem, closed with no hypotheses at the certified alternating group `A₅`, under `--cubical-compatible --exact-split --safe` with no postulates.

The companion paper would present the formalization to a type-theory and formalization audience, and it has its own mathematics to report, not just a port:

+  **A no-go theorem.**  Kurzweil surjectivity as classically stated (every subgroup in `[D , Sⁿ]` is a partition subgroup, with the partition produced as data) implies full excluded middle at level zero, via an oracle subgroup above the diagonal.  The classical lemma is therefore unprovable as stated in constructive type theory, and the restatement over decidably presented subgroups is forced, not stylistic.  This result is new, it is the paper's anchor, and it is outside the scope of *SmallLatticeReps*.
+  **A leaner proof than the literature's sketch.**  The formal proof does not use the unary-reduction theorem (`Con 𝑨 = Con ⟨A, Pol₁ 𝑨⟩`) that the informal argument cites: a self-contained translation criterion replaces it.  It also does not use the classification of normal subgroups of `Sⁿ` as partial products: a family of "killed projections" replaces it, keeping every simplicity application at the base-group level.  Formalization pressure produced a strictly smaller dependency graph, which is a point of independent interest.
+  **A transferable methodology.**  The assumption-registry discipline (classical imports as explicit hypotheses with calibrated logical strength, never postulates) and the two-layer semantic/decidable discipline, with oracle congruences and oracle subgroups as the systematic constructive obstruction.
+  **A documented AI-assisted workflow.**  The Agda was planned and composed with an AI assistant working through a typed-feedback loop (the agda-mcp server), with contemporaneous field reports.  A candid case study of this workflow on research-level mathematics is timely and, we believe, currently rare at this level of the literature.

**Division of labor with *SmallLatticeReps*** (to be agreed among the coauthors, and the main coordination point): *SmallLatticeReps* keeps the first full published informal proof and cites the formalization; the companion gives only the proof outline needed to make the formalization intelligible, cites *SmallLatticeReps* for the full informal treatment, and is anchored instead by the no-go theorem, the formalization itself, and the methodology.  With an agreed posting order, neither paper undercuts the other's claim.

## Proposed structure, with source map

Except where noted, the cited module headers are already written in publishable register (the library's corpus discipline requires literate prose around every code block), so much of the drafting is selection and adaptation rather than composition.

1.  **Introduction and history.**  Kurzweil's group-side theorem, Netter's unpublished generalization, Pálfy's 2009 lectures as the argument's transmission; the finite lattice representation problem as context; a contributions list.  *Sources*: `docs/papers/fin-lat-rep/SmallLatticeReps.tex` (section "Lattice duals"), DeMeo's thesis section 2.2, registry Entry 2 in `src/FLRP/Assumptions.lagda.md`.  *Caution*: every historical claim needs verification against Kurzweil (1985), which is in German and paywalled; see the checklist below.
2.  **The theorem and its proof, in outline.**  The three-stage organization: group side (the interval `[D , Sᵐ]` is dually `Eq(m)`, and the coset algebra realizes it as a congruence lattice), algebra side (congruences of a finite algebra as translation-invariant partitions), assembly (the expanded coset algebra).  *Sources*: the barrel narrative in `src/FLRP/KurzweilNetter.lagda.md`; full proof deferred to *SmallLatticeReps*.
3.  **The constructive frame.**  Safe Agda; the assumptions registry with calibrated strength; the two-layer discipline; the oracle-congruence no-go family (`chain₂` to weak excluded middle) that motivates it.  *Sources*: `src/FLRP/Assumptions.lagda.md` (header and Entry 1), `src/FLRP/Problem.lagda.md` (no-go section), ADR-008.
4.  **Kurzweil surjectivity is excluded middle at Layer S.**  The oracle subgroup, the no-go proof, the decidable restatement, and why the restatement loses nothing (the duality proof only ever applies surjectivity at base-coset classes of decidable congruences).  *Sources*: `src/FLRP/KurzweilNetter/Interval.lagda.md` (statement and no-go), pull requests [#568](https://github.com/ualib/agda-algebras/pull/568) and [#569](https://github.com/ualib/agda-algebras/pull/569).
5.  **The proof of surjectivity.**  The joint-kernel partition; killed projections as the recipients of every simplicity application; separation; the commutator support-shrinking iteration; block columns and the peeling induction; why the partial-products classification is deliberately avoided.  *Sources*: `src/Classical/Structures/Group/PowerCollapse.lagda.md` (whose header already contains the five-step outline), `src/Classical/Structures/Group/Commutator.lagda.md`, `src/FLRP/KurzweilNetter/Surjectivity.lagda.md`.
6.  **Assembling the duality, and the closed instantiation.**  The translation criterion replacing unary reduction; the expansion isomorphism; the measured type-checking economics of the composite-isomorphism glue (abstract parameters versus a three-minute renormalization); what "closed at `A₅`" means under `--safe`, and the certified simplicity of `A₅` by finite computation.  *Sources*: `src/FLRP/KurzweilNetter/{Translations,Expansion,Duality,A5}.lagda.md`, `src/Examples/Classical/Groups/AlternatingGroup5.lagda.md`.
7.  **How the proof was planned and composed.**  The plan-first workflow; the typed-feedback loop through the agda-mcp server; an error taxonomy from the sessions (what the type checker caught, and how fast); what the human directed versus what the assistant produced; an honest failure list (including a historical misattribution caught by the human author, which doubles as the argument for section 1's caution).  *Sources*: the field reports in the agda-native-air repository (`docs/mcp-field-reports.md`, entries dated 2026-09-01 and 2026-09-02), the pull-request bodies, session transcripts on request.  *Flag*: check target venues' policies on AI-generated text; the working stance is that the assistant is a tool, reported in the methodology, not an author.
8.  **Related work.**  The agda-algebras papers; formalizations of lattice theory and universal algebra in other proof assistants; constructive reverse mathematics of finite combinatorics; recent reports on AI-assisted formalization.  *Status*: candidates only; this section needs the human authors' scholarship most.
9.  **Conclusion and further work.**  The wreath-interval entry (Entry 5) and the RP-4 program as the natural continuation; what remains classical in the library and why.

## Facts to verify before any submission

+  The precise scope of Kurzweil (1985): which duality statement, and whether a solvability hypothesis is present (the registry's footnote says solvable intervals; the primary source must be read).
+  Netter (1986): exact title, status, and whether any copy is obtainable.
+  The Pálfy 2009 lecture citation, and his attribution of the argument.
+  Whether any prior machine-checked proof of the theorem, or of Kurzweil's lemma, exists (search the Isabelle AFP, mathlib, Mizar, and the Coq ecosystem).
+  The agreed division and posting order with *SmallLatticeReps*.

## Venues and timing

CPP 2027's deadline has passed (abstracts September 3, 2026).  Realistic targets, in the order we would try them: an arXiv preprint (math.RA or math.LO, cross-listed cs.LO) once the formalization is merged and the coauthors agree on the division; ITP 2027 (call not yet posted; deadlines historically in early spring); the Journal of Automated Reasoning (rolling) for a fuller treatment; TYPES 2027 with post-proceedings as a known route.  Gating order regardless of venue: the formalization merges to master first, then the coauthor agreement, then the preprint.

## Authorship, credit, and effort

Authorship is open, and the *SmallLatticeReps* coauthors are invited; the formalization sections would be drafted from the library's own prose and the session records, with the AI assistant's role described in the methodology section under the provenance conventions already used in this repository.  Estimated effort: a complete first draft is a few working sessions, because the source material above exists in near-publishable register; the human-expensive passes are related work, the historical checklist, and venue framing.  Working title candidates: "A machine-checked proof of the Kurzweil–Netter theorem", "Kurzweil–Netter, constructively: a formal proof and its classical price", "The Kurzweil–Netter theorem in safe Agda".
