# RP-4 design note: the wreath no-go and the dead-end question

This note records the design of research phase RP-4 (issue #461, `[M6-13j]`): the formalization of the note's Lemma 3.3 (`lem:IE-must-have-wreaths` of arXiv:1205.1927, vendored at `docs/papers/flrp/ieprops/`), the infrastructure it consumed, the index-hypothesis finding made while formalizing its proof, and the state of the phase's research question.  The reading assumes `docs/notes/flrp-research-roadmap.md` § 4 and the RP-1 note `docs/notes/flrp-rp1-parachutes.md`.

A terminological warning before anything else: two unrelated numbers in this phase are both called `n`.  The issue's "`n = 2` conjecture" is the *family-size-two* case of the empty-intersection question — a property and its negation.  The index-hypothesis finding of § 4 concerns the *coset index* `n = |G : H|` inside Kurzweil's construction.  This note says "the pair question" for the former and "index" only for the latter.

## 1.  What landed

+  `Classical.Structures.Group.Basic` gains the setoid-level builders `setoidOpsToBareGroup` and `setoidEqsToGroup`, mirroring `setoidEqsToLattice`; the wreath product is the first consumer.
+  `Classical.Structures.Group.IndexAction` — the `RightAction` record (a group acting on a bare index type up to `≡`, with congruence, identity, and contravariant compatibility), derived invertibility and injectivity, `Faithful`, the pointed coset-action specification `IsCosetAction` (basepoint, stabilizer both ways, reachability), and the **kernel–core correspondence** proved in both directions: for a coset action of `H`, faithfulness is equivalent to core-freeness of `H` (`coreFree→faithful`, `faithful→coreFree`).  This is the `ker φ = 1 ⟺ Core_G(H) = 1` step of the note's proof.
+  `Classical.Structures.Group.Wreath` — the permutational wreath product `S ≀ G = Sᴵ ⋊ G` over a `RightAction` (operator `_≀ᵍ_`), the diagonal-based subgroup `D Ḡ` (`Diag≀`, reusing the canonical `Diag` predicate), and the **core-freeness preservation theorem** `Diag≀-coreFree`: with a faithful action on at least two decidably-equal indices and a nontrivial centerless base group, `D Ḡ` is core-free in `S ≀ G`.  This is the technical heart of Lemma 3.3 — the step the note proves rather than cites — and the proof here repairs the note's index argument (§ 4).
+  `FLRP.WreathNoGo` — `HasTwoDistinct` and its definitional transport to the dual lattice; the Entry-5 statement type `KurzweilWreathInterval` with its data record `WreathIntervalData`; the derived `wreath-coreFree` (faithfulness from core-freeness, then preservation); **Lemma 3.3** as `cfIE-must-have-wreaths` by the double application; the omission corollary `omits-wreaths→not-cfIE`; the joint-wreath-richness constraint `contradictory-pair-wreaths`; the statement type `cfIE-no-contradictory-Statement` of the pair question; and the reduction `statement-C→no-contradictory-pair` placing the question below the parachute program's statement (C).
+  `FLRP.Assumptions` Entry 5 — `KurzweilWreathIntervalAt`, in the established per-entry style (meaning, bundling rationale, side conditions, split retirement path, layer).

## 2.  Decisions

### 2.1  The action is a right action on a bare index set

The note's display `(s , x)(t , y) = (s₁ t_{x(1)}, …, sₙ t_{x(n)}, x y)` reads coordinate `i` of a product as `s i ∙ t (x(i))`, and associativity of that multiplication is exactly the contravariant law `(x ∙ y)(i) = y(x(i))` — a right action written on the left.  The `RightAction` record states precisely that, plus a congruence field (the group carrier is a setoid; `≈`-equal elements must act identically) over an index type with propositional equality.  Nothing else is postulated: invertibility and injectivity of each `act x` are derived from the action laws, so no permutation-group object `Sym n`, no automorphism group `Aut(Sⁿ)`, and no bijectivity field exist anywhere in the construction.  This is the leanest interface that makes the wreath a group, and it keeps the eventual `Sym n` (which #502 may want as a bona fide group) an independent future module rather than a prerequisite.

### 2.2  The wreath is parameterized by the action, not by the coset space

Kurzweil's construction uses one specific action — `G` on the cosets of `H` — but building the coset space as a `Fin`-indexed carrier needs finite-index enumeration machinery the library lacks.  The construction therefore takes the action as a parameter, and the *tie* to `(G , H)` is the pointed specification `IsCosetAction`: the basepoint's stabilizer is `H` in both directions, and every index is reachable.  That specification is exactly strong enough to *prove* the kernel–core correspondence, so faithfulness of the packaged action is derived from core-freeness of the representation rather than assumed — the formal division of labor the phase brief asked for ("`ker φ = 1 ⟺ H` core-free").

### 2.3  Operations pointwise, laws coordinatewise, builders at the setoid level

The wreath twist is not coordinatewise in the product of the two carriers, so the term-transfer route of `_×ᵍ_` and `⨅ᵍ` (satisfaction transported along factors) is unavailable; the five group laws are proved by hand.  Each is a named lemma whose base component reduces to the base group's law after rewriting tuple indices along one action law — `act-∙` for associativity, `act-ε` for the left identity, the derived `act-invˡ` for the right inverse — and whose top component is the top group's law.  The new `setoidEqsToGroup` assembles the group; its interpretation clauses apply the argument tuple directly, so every law's statement evaluates definitionally to its curried form (the same reduction discipline as `eqsToGroup` and `setoidEqsToLattice`).

### 2.4  `D Ḡ` reuses the canonical diagonal, and one implicit must be forwarded

Membership in `D Ḡ` is `Diag` of the base component — the library keeps a single diagonal notion, and the `Diag` predicate of `Classical.Structures.Group.Diagonal` applies verbatim because the power's carrier and the wreath's base component are the same function type.  One wrinkle worth recording: the multiplication-closure lemma's type never mentions the right factor's *top* component (membership factors through `proj₁`, and the product's base twists only by the left top), so passing it to `mkIsSubgroup` with implicit pair arguments leaves that component an unsolved metavariable; the closure is stated with explicit pairs and applied through a wrapper that forwards them.  This is the same phenomenon `FLRP.Enforceable` records at its `from-mono`, and the fix follows the session-memory rule "inline or forward endpoints".

### 2.5  Probe tuples are the block indicators of the partition-subgroup module

The core-freeness proof conjugates by elements `(t , ε)` whose tuple is supported at a single index (`probe j a`, with `dec-true` / `dec-false` discharging the support equations) — the same single-index indicator idiom as `Classical.Structures.Group.PartitionSubgroup`'s order-reflection tuples, and the only place decidable index equality enters.  The one case split in the whole development (is the index moved?) is passed to a named lemma taking the `Dec` value as an argument, per the repository's `with`-discipline.

### 2.6  The double dual is definitional at the order level

`dualLattice` re-interprets the two operation symbols by the swapped operations on the same carrier setoid; dualizing twice therefore restores the original interpretations *definitionally*, and since `IntervalIso` reads only the carrier setoid and the meet order, the second application's interval isomorphism — typed against `dualLattice (dualLattice 𝑳)` — is consumed at type `IntervalIso … 𝑳` with no transport, adapter, or coercion.  This validates the design remark in `Classical.Structures.Lattice.Dual` (involutivity was deliberately not formalized because consumers would not need it) at its first real consumer: Lemma 3.3 type-checks with `K₂.interval` passed as-is.

### 2.7  Namespace and level discipline

Everything reusable — builders, actions, wreaths, both core-freeness theorems — lives under `Classical/Structures/Group/` at polymorphic levels; `FLRP/` holds only the `0ℓ`-pinned assembly, the registry entry, and the research-question statements, per roadmap § 6.  The Classical theorems state core-freeness as `proj₁ core ⊆ proj₁ (trivialSubgroup _)`, which is definitionally what `CoreFree` of `FLRP.Enforceable` unfolds to, so the FLRP side consumes them with no adapters.

## 3.  What is assumed, and why

Exactly one thing is imported: **Entry 5**, `KurzweilWreathIntervalAt 𝒮` — from a core-free representation `[H , G] ≅ 𝑳` with `𝑳` two-element-distinct, Kurzweil's construction yields the enumerated coset action (a `RightAction` on `Fin (2 + m)` satisfying `IsCosetAction` for `H`) and the interval isomorphism `[D Ḡ , 𝒮 ≀ G] ≅ 𝑳′`.  The entry deliberately bundles two steps of different characters, and the registry documents the split: the interval isomorphism is the real citation (Kurzweil 1985, the article behind Entries 2 and 4; the note cites it without reproof at exactly this point), while the existence of the enumerated coset action is elementary finiteness bookkeeping awaiting infrastructure — provable mathematics, not an imported theorem.  Nothing else is assumed.  In particular the entry makes no faithfulness or core-freeness claims (both are proved), and the side conditions — `𝒮` finite nonabelian simple, `G` finite — stay in prose exactly as Entry 4's simplicity condition does, pending issue #512.  The hypotheses `NontrivialCenterless 𝒮` threaded through the FLRP results are the two *formal fragments* of nonabelian simplicity the proofs consume, discharged by any consumer at instantiation; they are hypotheses in the ordinary sense, not registry entries.

The two-distinct-elements hypothesis on `𝑳` is *necessary*, not defensive: a trivial lattice forces `H = G`, a one-point coset space, and `D Ḡ = S ≀ G`, whose core is everything — and Lemma 3.3 itself is false at the one-point lattice (cf-IE via it constrains only the trivial group, so "is trivial" is cf-IE by a group-representable lattice yet contains no wreath product).  See § 4.

## 4.  The index-hypothesis finding

The phase brief flagged the note's justification of its index bound for checking, and formalization settled it.  The note's proof of core-freeness preservation picks, for a moved index `x(1) = j ≠ 1`, a third index `k ∉ {1 , j}`, justified by: "this is possible since `n = |G : H| > 2`; for otherwise `H ⊴ G`, contradicting `Core_G(H) = 1`."  Two separate things are wrong-or-repairable here, and one hypothesis is genuinely missing from the lemma's statement:

+  **The justification is incomplete at `H = 1`.**  Index `2` does force `H ⊴ G` and hence `Core_G(H) = H` — but that contradicts core-freeness only when `H ≠ 1`.  The pair `(H , G) = (1 , C₂)` is core-free with index exactly `2`, so the claimed `n > 2` does not follow; it fails precisely when the given representation is `[1 , C₂]`, i.e. a two-element interval.  (Likewise `(1 , 1)` has index `1`.)  Since the note's proof runs on an arbitrarily given core-free representation of `L`, the argument as written has a gap whenever that representation is `[1 , C₂]`.
+  **The conclusion survives; the proof is repaired by removing the third index.**  Probing the constancy constraint at the two indices `i₀` and `j = x(i₀)` with the tuple that is `a` at `j` and identity elsewhere yields `d ∙ a ⁻¹ ≈ a ∙ d` for every `a` (the probe vanishes at `i₀`, and at `x(j)`, which avoids the support by injectivity of `x`).  Conjugation by `d` is then an inversion, which forces any two elements of `S` to commute — `(p ∙ q) = d (p ∙ q)⁻¹ d⁻¹ = (d q⁻¹ d⁻¹)(d p⁻¹ d⁻¹) = q ∙ p` — contradicting a nontrivial centerless (hence nonabelian) base.  One uniform argument, no case analysis on whether `x` is a transposition, no lower bound on the index beyond *two points*; formalized as `moved→inv-conj` and `inv-conj→comm`, it makes the preservation theorem hold at index `2`, where the note's own argument did not reach.
+  **The lemma needs a nontriviality hypothesis that the note leaves implicit.**  At index `1` — the representation `[1 , 1]` of the one-element lattice — preservation is genuinely false: every one-index tuple is diagonal, `D Ḡ` is all of `S ≀ G`, and its core is everything.  Consequently Lemma 3.3 itself fails at the one-element lattice: `𝒢₀(1-chain) = {(1 , 1)}`, so the property "is trivial" is cf-IE by a group-representable lattice and contains no wreath product.  The formal statements therefore carry `HasTwoDistinct 𝑳`, which classically is exactly `|G : H| ≥ 2` and is also what Entry 5's `Fin (2 + m)` records.

Summary: the note's Lemma 3.3 is *true* for every lattice with two distinct elements, *false* at the one-element lattice, and its proof's index argument is incomplete at the representation `[1 , C₂]` but repairable by an argument that is also simpler than the original.  This is a textbook instance of what formalization is for, and it is filed as an erratum issue against the vendored note (see § 6).

## 5.  The pair question: what is settled, and what remains open

RP-4's research question — the pair question — asks whether a property and its negation can both be cf-IE by group-representable lattices.  It is *stated* formally (`cfIE-no-contradictory-Statement`, in the vacuity-disciplined form where each lattice comes with a core-free representation) and deliberately not asserted.  What this phase settles around it:

+  **Both members of a contradictory pair are wreath-rich** (`contradictory-pair-wreaths`): over every admissible `𝒮`, the class of `P` and the class of `¬ P` each contain wreath products `𝒮 ≀ Ū` — with different `Ū`, which is exactly why no contradiction ensues.  The plain-IE no-go (Lemma 3.2) merged its two witnesses by fattening, and fattening destroys core-freeness; at the cf-IE level the merge is the entire difficulty.  Any refutation of the pair question must therefore separate the two classes by invariants finer than wreath content — on the menu supplied by RP-1's Lemma 3.7 for parachute representations: the unique minimal normal subgroup, the vanishing of its centralizer, and the permutation action on it.
+  **The omission corollary** (`omits-wreaths→not-cfIE`) is the catalog-facing positive yield: classes omitting wreath products over some admissible simple base — solvable groups, alternating/symmetric groups, almost simple groups — are not cf-IE by any lattice carrying a core-free representation and two distinct elements.  This is the note's partial answer to the pair question, now formal.
+  **The question sits below statement (C)** (`statement-C→no-contradictory-pair`): the parachute statement (C) of `FLRP.Enforceable`, instantiated at the two-element family `(𝑳₁ , 𝑳₂)` with properties `(P , ¬ P)`, produces a single group satisfying both, refuting the pair.  Contrapositively, a contradictory pair refutes (C), hence (B) through the RP-1 meta-theorem, hence — with Entry 3 (Pálfy–Pudlák) — the FLRP itself.  So a *positive* answer to the pair question is a concrete route to a negative FLRP answer, and a *negative* answer (a proof of `cfIE-no-contradictory-Statement`) is implied by (B): the pair question is squeezed between the program's two possible outcomes, which is precisely why it is the dead-end branch.

The reduction to named open questions, per the issue's exit criterion, is then: (i) **statement (B)** of Pálfy–Pudlák — open since 1980 — implies the pair question resolves negatively, via (B) → (C) (formal modulo the `ParachuteHypotheses` record of RP-1) and `statement-C→no-contradictory-pair` (formal); (ii) exhibiting a contradictory pair requires two cf-IE classes separated by post-wreath invariants, for which no candidate construction is known — the constraints a candidate must satisfy are Lemma 3.3 (this phase, formal) and Lemma 3.7's forcing (RP-1, formal); (iii) two bookkeeping gaps stand between the formal reduction and its classical reading: the finite-presentation transport recorded open in the RP-1 note § 4, and the side-condition mismatch that (C) asks three distinct elements per canopy while the pair question needs only two — a contradictory pair living exclusively on two-element chains would evade the reduction as formalized (classically no such pair exists: cf-IE via a two-element chain forces the property on every primitive permutation group, and those wreath-rich classes intersect; recording this properly is catalog material for RP-2).

Nothing beyond this is claimed.  In particular this phase produces no structure theorem on empty-intersection families beyond joint wreath-richness, and the sobering asymmetry of the roadmap stands: the existence of such families cannot be ruled out short of proving (B).
