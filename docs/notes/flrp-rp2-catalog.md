# RP-2 survey note: the enforcement catalog

Research phase RP-2 (GitHub [Issue #459](https://github.com/ualib/agda-algebras/issues/459)) builds the machine-readable catalog of "an interval of this shape forces a group of this kind" theorems, each recast as a precise (cf-/min-)interval-enforceability statement.

The formal artifact is [`src/FLRP/Reductions.lagda.md`](../../src/FLRP/Reductions.lagda.md), supported by the new reusable module [`src/Classical/Structures/Group/MinimalNormal.lagda.md`](../../src/Classical/Structures/Group/MinimalNormal.lagda.md); this note is its companion, consisting of the entry table, the verification status of every literature claim, the formalization decisions, and the entries considered and rejected.

The framework the catalog is written in is RP-1's (`docs/notes/flrp-rp1-parachutes.md`, `FLRP.Enforceable`, `FLRP.Parachute*`); the program that consumes it is RP-3's hunt for cf-IE classes with empty intersection (roadmap § 4).

+  `witness→¬IE` / `witness→¬cfIE`, refutation of enforcement from a representation inside the complementary class; this is what makes negative entries (Entry 8) first-class.
+  `cfIE→¬¬` and `cfIE→IE`, **Lemma 3.1 of the note, proved** (RP-1 left it as the statement type `cfIE→IE-Statement`), together with `negation-Stable` and `HClosed→ComplementHClosed`.
+  `CoreFreeExclusion`, `exclusion→cfIE`, `exclusion→IE`, the common shape of Entries 4 and 5.
+  `MinimallyIE`, `IE→MinimallyIE`, `MinimallyIE-∧`, and `minIE-degenerate`, the min-IE layer, repaired (§ 4.7).
+  `IE-family→cfIE-family` and the re-exported `conjunction-cfIE`, `empty-intersection→not-representable`, `strategy-meta-theorem`, composition, in the `Compose` submodule.

## 2.  How the literature was read

`arxiv.org` and `api.semanticscholar.org` are reachable from the fetch tool; `en.wikipedia.org` and `ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org` are not.  Two mechanical points worth recording, because they changed what could be verified.

+  The fetch tool cannot extract text from PDFs, but `curl` in the shell reaches `arxiv.org/e-print/<id>` and `arxiv.org/pdf/<id>`, and `pdftotext -layout` (in the Nix shell) turns both into greppable text.  Every primary-source quotation in § 3 was read that way: Basile's thesis and DeMeo's thesis as PDFs, the Lucchini–Moscatiello–Palcoux–Spiga paper as its LaTeX source.
+  `curl` also reaches `en.wikipedia.org` and `ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org` (both answer `200`), so the restriction is in the fetch tool's domain policy, not in the environment's egress.  Until that policy changes, blocked-domain material can be read through the shell.
+  **Dropped symbols (M6-27, 2026-09-28)**.  `pdftotext -layout` on the JAMS PDF drops every `≠` and `≰` in Sections 4 and 6 of the 2008 paper (the glyphs come from a font with no Unicode mapping), so `M₂ ∩ M′ ≠ H` reads as `M₂ ∩ M′ = H`, silently.  The misreading in the first version of § 4.12 came from exactly this; the repair was to render the pages (`pdftoppm -r 105 -png`) and read the images for every inequality that carries weight.  Do the same for any extracted text whose argument turns on a negated relation.
+  **Kjos-Hanssen's note and the closure literature (M6-27, 2026-09-28)**.  `CLFA8.pdf`, William's copy, read in full through `pdftotext`.  Snow 2000 was obtained later the same day (`2000-Snow-ConstructiveApproach.pdf`, supplied by William, with two further Snow papers) and read in full: its parallel sum `L ∓ N` adjoins a new top and a new bottom, so Lemmas 3.9 and 3.10 do not give the glued operation Kjos-Hanssen's note describes, and a parachute is not a parallel sum (`docs/notes/flrp-lattices8-census.md`).  The fin-lat-rep manuscript (`docs/papers/fin-lat-rep/SmallLatticeReps.tex`) and DeMeo's thesis were read for the minimal-representation theorems.
+  **The Aschbacher texts (M6-21, 2026-09-28)**.  The journal sites of the AMS and Elsevier answer `curl` with a bot challenge, whatever headers it sends, and the Chrome extension was not connected; the texts came from elsewhere.  CaltechAUTHORS (`authors.library.caltech.edu`, an InvenioRDM instance with a JSON API at `/api/records`) holds the published PDFs of the 2008 JAMS paper (record `cm5e5-ptt87`) and the 2012 J. Group Theory paper (record `9saf3-dsg11`), downloadable with `?download=1`; Project Euclid serves the 2009 Michigan paper's PDF directly at its DOI path; Crossref supplies every DOI; and the zbMATH Open API (`api.zbmath.org/v1/document/_search`) returns the reviews of the two Elsevier papers, which state their main theorems.  Later the same day William downloaded the three J. ### The one scope gap, stated rather than papered over

Basile's Theorem D is about alternating and symmetric groups of degree at least 5.  Entry 5's imported hypothesis (`AltSymExclusion`) is stated for the whole class, so it silently covers degrees `≤ 4` as well.  Those cases are true by inspection, and the inspection is worth reporting because it is *not* vacuous.  An interval isomorphic to `Mₙ` over `H ≤ G` needs `n` subgroups maximal in `G` with `H` maximal in each.  `A₄` has only five maximal subgroups in all (`V₄` and four `C₃`'s), so six is impossible.  `S₄` has eight (`A₄`, three `D₄`'s, four `S₃`'s), and since a maximal subgroup of `S₄` has order 12, 8, or 6, such an `H` has order 2, 3, or 4: the four-element candidates are `V₄`, which is maximal in `A₄` and in each `D₄` and in nothing else (four overgroups), and the three `C₄`'s, each inside one `D₄`; a transposition lies in two `S₃`'s and one `D₄`; a double transposition lies in `A₄` and the three `D₄`'s but is not maximal in `A₄`; a `C₃` lies in `A₄` and one `S₃`.  So the maximum is four, attained by `[V₄ , S₄] ≅ M₄`.

Two things follow.  `M₆` does not occur below degree 5, so Entry 5's hypothesis as stated is true, but `M₄` *does* occur, in the symmetric group `S₄`, so the smallest `Mₙ` outside Basile's list of counts would **not** have served: the choice of `M₆` is load-bearing, and a catalog that had reached for `M₄` would have recorded a false entry.  This inspection was done by hand while writing this note and is **not machine-checked**; the honest ways to close it are to state the hypothesis with the degree restriction and add "every alternating or symmetric group of degree `≤ 4` is solvable" as a second named hypothesis (composing Entry 5 with Entry 4), or to decide the small cases by computation once the library has `Aₙ`/`Sₙ`.  The gap is recorded here, in the module's prose, and in the issue.

## 4.  Formalization decisions

### 4.1  Subdirect irreducibility is stated group-side; the bridge was a follow-up, since landed

At the time of the decision the library had no correspondence between the normal subgroups of a group and its congruences, so `Setoid.Congruences.Monolith.IsSubdirectlyIrreducible`, which is about `Con 𝑨`, could not be applied to a group without building that bridge.  The bridge has since landed; the update at the end of this section records what it delivered and what remains.  The rest of this section is the decision as it was taken.  RP-1 proves subdirect irreducibility in its constructive *pairwise* form (`Minimal.normals-meet`: no nontrivial normal subgroup meets the minimal normal subgroup trivially).

The decision: state `𝒢₂` group-side, as `HasMonolithᵍ` of the new `Classical.Structures.Group.MinimalNormal`, and record the divergence.  Three reasons.

+  It is the note's own definition: "Recall, for groups *subdirectly irreducible* is equivalent to having a unique minimal normal subgroup."  So the group-side form is not an approximation of the entry, it *is* the entry.
+  RP-2's job is to state literature facts precisely, not to build infrastructure; the bridge was reusable mathematics belonging in `Classical/Structures/Group/` beside `FLRP.Bridge`'s Pálfy–Pudlák correspondence, with its own issue.
+  The group-side statement was strengthened to the shape the algebra-side notion has, so the bridge would be a *transport* and not a reproof: `minimal-meets→least` upgrades RP-1's pairwise form to the least-element form of `IsMonolith.mono-least`, constructively (`M ∩ N` is a normal subgroup inside `M`, and it is nontrivial exactly because `M` and `N` do not meet trivially, so minimality gives `M ⊆ M ∩ N ⊆ N`).

What the bridge needed, as recorded before it was built: from a normal subgroup `N`, the relation `x θ y ⟺ x y⁻¹ ∈ N` is a congruence of the group algebra; from a congruence `θ`, the class `{x : x θ ε}` is a normal subgroup; the two maps are mutually inverse and monotone, so they are an order isomorphism `Con 𝑮 ≅ Normal(𝑮)`; nontriviality corresponds to nonzeroness on the nose.  With it, `HasMonolithᵍ` transports to `HasMonolith` and `𝒢₂` becomes the library's `IsSubdirectlyIrreducible`.  It was ordinary work (no obstruction, no classical input) and, once the monolith instance is transported, it retires the `ᵍ` superscript.

### 4.2  Minimal-normal descent is threaded, not dropped

RP-1's Lemma 3.7 machinery takes a *minimal normal subgroup* as a module parameter, because its existence follows from finiteness by well-founded descent, which the library does not yet have.  Entries 1–3 must therefore be honest about it, and the tempting shortcut (stating the classes only for groups that come equipped with a minimal normal subgroup) would quietly weaken the quantifier over normal subgroups.

The decision: the hypothesis is a *named property of the group being constrained*, `MinimalNormalDescent` ("every nontrivial normal subgroup contains a minimal one"), and it appears as the **antecedent of the enforced property**:

```agda
entry-𝒢₄ : cfIE (λ 𝒢 → MinimalNormalDescent 𝒢 → 𝒢₄ 𝒢) ⊕ᵖ-Lattice
```

So the entry says: *core-free interval enforceability, via the parachute, of "if descent holds then every nontrivial normal subgroup has trivial centralizer"*.  The quantifier over normal subgroups is intact, the class is the note's, classically every finite group satisfies the antecedent, and `cfIE` is untouched, so the entry still composes with `conjunction-cfIE` and everything else in the catalog's vocabulary.  At the time of the decision this shape was expected to retire outright once descent became a theorem for finite groups; the update below records what the theorem actually left standing, and why the antecedent survives in presentational form.

**Update (M6-18, #510)**.  Descent is now a theorem (`Classical.Structures.Group.MinimalNormalDescent`, and `finite-MinimalNormalDescent` of `FLRP.Reductions` discharges this very property), but the antecedent does not disappear, and the reason is worth recording because it was not anticipated when the decision above was taken.  `MinimalNormalDescent` demands minimality against *every* normal subgroup, with nontriviality in the negative form `¬ (N ⊆ 1)`, and its **witnessed** reading is **classical**: `minimal→DNE` shows an unrestricted-minimal normal subgroup of a finite group, together with a witnessed non-identity element, decides `¬ ¬ P → P` for every proposition at the working level, by an oracle subgroup `M ∩ (1 ∪ P)` in the style of the WP-1 oracle congruence, so no proof of descent can hand back witnessed minimal subgroups unconditionally, and some presentation datum is unavoidable on the route every consumer uses.  What is unconditional is the *witnessed* form, whose minimality clause still ranges over all normal subgroups and restricts only the shape of their nontriviality hypothesis.  So the antecedent survives, but changes character: it is now `DecidablyPresented` (the group-side reading of `complete` of `FiniteCongruences`, the library's one Layer-S bridge) rather than an unproved statement of finite group theory.  See `docs/notes/m6-18-minimal-normal-descent.md` § 3.

### 4.2a  Entries 2 and 3 are derived from the *general* centralizer fact, not from `Minimal.nonabelian`

RP-1 proves `Minimal.nonabelian`, which says the *minimal normal subgroup* `M` fixed as a module parameter is nonabelian.  The note's `𝒢₃` is stronger (*every* nontrivial normal subgroup is nonabelian), so the entry is derived instead from `Structure.centralizer-of-normal` (RP-1's antitone extension of `Minimal.centralizer-trivial`) together with `abelian→⊆-centralizer`: given a nontrivial normal `N`, descent supplies a minimal normal subgroup inside it, `centralizer-of-normal` makes `C_G(N)` trivial, and an abelian `N` would sit inside `C_G(N)`.  `Minimal.nonabelian` is then the special case `N = M`, so nothing is lost and the quantifier is the note's.  Entry 3 (`𝒢₄`) comes from the same `centralizer-of-normal`, and Entry 1 (`𝒢₂`) from `Minimal.normals-meet` through `minimal-meets→least`.

### 4.3  `𝒢₃` is stated contrapositively

"No nontrivial abelian normal subgroup" reads naturally as "every abelian normal subgroup is trivial", but that form is not derivable from the centralizer argument without deciding triviality: the argument gives `¬ ¬ (N ⊆ 1)`, and `N ⊆ 1` is a Π-statement whose stability needs the group's equality to be stable.  The note's own Remark states the contrapositive, "every nontrivial normal subgroup of `G` is nonabelian", and that is what `𝒢₃` is.  Same statement classically, no decision needed, and it is the form the proof produces.  This follows RP-1 § 3.2 (`normal-in-proper-trivial`), where the same choice was made for `NY = G`.

### 4.4  `Mₙ` is the parachute of `n` two-element chains

The enforcing lattices of Entries 4–6 are the height-two lattices `Mₙ`, and they need no new construction: `Mₙ = 𝒫(𝟚 , … , 𝟚)`.  The parachute's carrier is the shared top, the fresh bottom, and the *proper* elements of each canopy; a two-element chain has exactly one proper element, its bottom, which is the `n`-th atom.  So `M[ n ]` is definitionally the right lattice, with `ParachuteAtoms.atom` and `ParachuteAtoms.covered` witnessing the height-two shape, and `chain₂-top`/`chain₂-bot` (already in `FLRP.Closure.Basic`) supplying the extremum data.

A pleasing consequence, and a genuine structural fact rather than a coincidence: **`Mₙ` has no big canopy**, so the note's hypothesis "at least two `|Lᵢ| > 2`" fails and *none* of the parachute theorems applies to it.  That is exactly why Entries 4–6 need external theorems where Entries 1–3 need only RP-1, and the formal statement of the distinction is the `BigCanopyᴸ` argument the parachute entries take and the `Mₙ` entries cannot supply.

### 4.5  Lemma 3.1 is proved, and it is classical-free for every entry the catalog has

RP-1 left the note's Lemma 3.1 as an uninhabited statement type.  It is proved here in six lines (`cfIE→IE`), and the proof splits cleanly: the constructive core `cfIE→¬¬` needs only the core-free reduction and H-closure of the complementary class, and the classical step is isolated in the `PropertyStable` hypothesis.

The observation that makes this more than bookkeeping: every cf-IE property the literature supplies is the **negation** of a common group property (the note remarks on this), and a negation is double-negation stable with no assumption whatsoever (`negation-Stable`).  So for Entries 4 and 5 the upgrade from cf-IE to IE costs *nothing classical* beyond the core-free reduction and H-closure, and the note's own reading of Lemma 3.1 as a classical step is unnecessarily pessimistic for the cases it is applied to.

### 4.6  Exclusion is the shape the sources actually deliver

Both Entries 4 and 5 come from theorems of the form "no group in class `Q` has `𝑳` above a core-free subgroup".  `CoreFreeExclusion Q 𝑳` names that shape, `exclusion→cfIE` observes that it *is* cf-IE of `¬ Q`, and `exclusion→IE` upgrades it.  Two consequences worth recording: the two entries share all their reasoning, so a third exclusion lattice costs one hypothesis and no proof; and the sources' *unrestricted* statements (Pálfy–Pudlák's solvable theorem, as quoted, has no core-freeness hypothesis) are strictly stronger than what the entry consumes, so the entry is stated at the weaker hypothesis and the upgrade is available either way.

### 4.7  `minIE` of `FLRP.Enforceable` is degenerate, and the catalog repairs it

WP-4's definition quantifies minimality against a *single* other representation:

```agda
minIE P 𝑳 = ∀ 𝒢 𝒬 H J H-sg J-sg → (fin : FiniteAlgebra …) → IntervalIso 𝒢 H H-sg 𝑳
           → (fin' : FiniteAlgebra …) → IntervalIso 𝒬 J J-sg 𝑳 → fin .card ≤ⁿ fin' .card → P 𝒢
```

Instantiating `𝒬 := 𝒢` and `fin' := fin` makes the cardinality premise `card ≤ card`, so `minIE P 𝑳` implies `P` of **every** finitely presented representation of `𝑳`: it is plain interval enforceability restricted to finite groups, not minimal enforceability.  The one-line proof is `minIE-degenerate`, kept in the module as the record of the defect.

The catalog therefore states Entry 6 over `MinimallyIE`, in which minimality is quantified over all finite representations.  `minIE` should be retired in favour of it; FLRP modules are exempt from the deprecation cycle (roadmap § 1), so the replacement can be direct.  This is a follow-up against `FLRP.Enforceable`, deliberately not done on this branch because PR #506 is in review and changes to that file are being kept additive.

### 4.8  Solvability and `Aₙ`/`Sₙ` are abstract predicates

The library defines neither, so Entries 4, 5, 7, and 8 are *schemas*: each takes the predicate as a module parameter together with the facts its sources supply about it.  Nothing is lost (the schemas instantiate unchanged when the predicates land) and something is gained: the entries make explicit exactly which properties of "solvable" are used (H-closure, and the exclusion), which is a shorter list than one would guess.

### 4.9  Negative entries are first-class

Entry 8 records that a lattice does **not** enforce a property.  That is not a defect of the catalog but part of its job: RP-3 searches over enforcing lattices, and knowing that rank-three Boolean lattices cannot enforce `𝒢₁`, because Lucchini–Moscatiello–Palcoux–Spiga *realize* such intervals inside alternating and symmetric groups, prunes the search.  `witness→¬IE` is the operation, and it is the exact dual of the vacuity theorem: a representation inside the class refutes enforcement, a representation of the lattice anywhere refutes vacuity.

DeMeo's Theorem 6.3.1 has six clauses; the entry imports four. ### 4.11  Layer discipline: the catalog inherits WP-4's interval presentation and needs no decisions

ADR-008's two-layer discipline concerns congruences (Layer S) versus decidable congruences (Layer D), and on the group side the same split appears as `Interval≈` versus `Intervalᵈ` in `FLRP.Enforceable`. ### 4.12  Aschbacher's program, specialized to parachutes

Issue M6-21 asked what Aschbacher's `D∆` and signalizer theorems say about minimal parachute representations, once the primary texts are in hand.  All five texts were read, three in full and the 2012 and 2013 papers in their introductions and first sections (§ 2, § 3); this is what they say, with the vocabulary defined on first use.

**The classes**.  For a finite lattice `Λ` with least element `0` and greatest element `∞`, write `Λ′ = Λ − {0 , ∞}` and regard it as a graph whose adjacency is comparability.  An element `m` is *modular* when `(a ∨ m) ∧ b = a ∨ (m ∧ b)` for all `a ≤ b`.  Aschbacher 2008 defines the following classes.

+  **A-lattice**: `|Λ| > 2` and `0`, `∞` are the only modular elements.
+  **D-lattice**: `Λ′` admits a partition `Λ₁ ∪ Λ₂` with each `Λᵢ` a union of connected components (D1) and each containing a nontrivial chain `k < m` (D2).  His lemma (1.2): D-lattices are A-lattices.
+  **`C*`-lattice** (coatomistic): every element of `Λ′` is a meet of coatoms; **`C∗`-lattice** (atomistic): the dual; **C-lattice**: both; **CD-lattice**: C and D.
+  **`D(m₁ , … , mₜ)`-lattice** (2009), written `DΔ(m₁ , … , mₜ)` in the 2009 Aschbacher–Shareshian paper and reviews of it: `Λ′` has `t > 1` connected components, the `i`-th isomorphic to the proper part of the Boolean lattice of rank `mᵢ ≥ 3`.  These are CD-lattices; Shareshian's Conjecture D says none is a finite group interval lattice.

**The dictionary for parachutes**.  The proper part of `𝒫(L₁ , … , Lₙ)` is the disjoint union of the canopies with the shared top removed, and two proper elements are comparable only inside a canopy, so the components of `Λ′` are the canopies, each connected through its atom.  Hence, for `n ≥ 2`:

+  the parachute is a **D-lattice iff at least two canopies are big** (`|Lᵢ| > 2`): a canopy carries a nontrivial chain exactly when it has an element strictly between its atom and the top, and the partition puts one big canopy on each side;
+  it is an **A-lattice iff it is a D-lattice**: with no big canopy every atom is modular (`Mₙ` is modular), and with exactly one the big canopy's atom is modular (check the pairs `a < b`, which all lie in that canopy);
+  it is **never atomistic** when some canopy is big (a join of atoms from two canopies is the top, so a proper element is a join of atoms only if it is an atom), hence **never a CD-lattice**, and neither is its dual, which fails the coatomistic condition by the mirror argument;
+  it is coatomistic iff every canopy is coatomistic including its bottom (the atom must be a meet of that canopy's coatoms), which chains fail and `Mₖ` and Boolean canopies satisfy;
+  `DΔ(m₁ , … , mₜ)` is the parachute of `t` Boolean lattices **with its `t` atoms deleted**; conversely no `DΔ`-lattice is a parachute, since its components have no least element.

So the note's hypothesis for Theorem 3.6 and Lemma 3.7, "at least two `|Lᵢ| > 2`", is exactly "the parachute is a D-lattice".  This is formalized: `Classical.Structures.Lattice.Disconnected` defines modular elements, `IsALattice` (contrapositively: no proper element is modular), the D-partition as a two-coloring constant across comparable proper pairs, and proves `D→A` and `parachute-DPartition` (two big canopies give a D-partition, the canopy index as the color).

**Where the two structure theories compose**.  Aschbacher's Proposition 2 (2008): if `H` is core-free in `G` and `O_G(H)` is an A-lattice, then (1) `G` has a unique minimal normal subgroup `D`; (2) `G = HD`; (3) `D` is the direct product of the components of `G`, nonabelian simple groups permuted transitively by `H`; (4) `U ↦ U ∩ D` is a poset isomorphism of `O_G(H)` onto the `H`-invariant subgroups of `D` containing `H ∩ D`.  On a parachute with two big canopies this is Lemma 3.7 of the note (2012) and more: (1) is `𝒢₂`, (1) with (3) gives `𝒢₃` and `𝒢₄`, and (2) is `NH = G`, all of which RP-1 derives; what Proposition 2 adds is the **transitivity** of `H` on the components of the monolith (an invariant of the action, which is exactly the F10 direction the RP-3 note asks for) and the **presentation of the interval inside the monolith** by (4), which is elementary ((2.1) in the paper is Dedekind's rule applied to `G = HN`) and derivable from RP-1's `NH-all`.  Proposition 2 is Entry 10, imported for A-lattices in general and instantiated on parachutes, where it agrees with Entries 1 through 3.  Two consequences are recorded rather than formalized: the transitivity clause needs the component decomposition of a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup, which the library does not have, and (4) is a statement about the pair `(H , G)`, not a group property, so it is catalog *vocabulary* rather than an entry.

**Where they do not, as first read; corrected by M6-27**.  The reductions the issue hoped for, "a minimal representation is almost simple or a signalizer lattice arises", are Theorem 3 of the 2008 paper (for CD-lattices) and Theorem 2 of the 2009 paper (for `D(m₁ , … , mₜ)`-lattices), and neither is *stated* for any parachute with a big canopy, because such a parachute is not a CD-lattice: Aschbacher's components are atomistic and coatomistic with no least element, and the parachute construction *adds* a least element to each component.  The first version of this paragraph went on to say that the two places his proof uses the C condition both fail for parachutes, and that was half wrong.  His (6.5) rules out the case in which every maximal overgroup is of product type by passing to the smaller group `Aut_G(L)` with the same interval, which needs the coatomistic condition ((4.12)(7)); a parachute has it only when every canopy is coatomistic including its bottom.  His (6.6)(3) needs two coatoms of one component meeting *above* `H` (`M₂ ∩ M′ ≠ H`, p. 825; the extracted text had dropped the symbol, § 2, and the first reading took the step to need `M₂ ∩ M′ = H`), and two coatoms of one canopy always meet above `H`, since they meet above the canopy's atom.  So for a coatomistic parachute with two big canopies his Section 6 goes through, with one caveat about the dual; that is Entry 13, § 4.13, and `docs/notes/flrp-parachute-theorem3.md`.  The mismatch that remains is the one recorded here before: Aschbacher's minimality is over representations of `Λ` **or its dual** (`G*(Λ)`), while the catalog's `MinimallyIE` quantifies over representations of `Λ` alone; Entry 13 states both minimality notions and derives a corollary for each.

**What the texts give instead**.  Section 8 of the 2008 paper is a construction of two-canopy parachutes: under his Hypothesis 8.2 (`H = H₁ × H₂` with `Hᵢ` nonabelian simple, `Kᵢ ≤ Hᵢ` isomorphic to a simple `L`, `N_{Hᵢ}(Kᵢ) = Kᵢ`, and no nontrivial `Kᵢ`-invariant subgroup of `Hᵢ` meeting `Kᵢ` trivially), his (8.4) says `O_G(H)` for the group `G(τ)` of his Section 7 is the parachute whose canopies are the duals of `[K₁ , H₁]` and `[K₂ , H₂]` with a new top adjoined.  Example 8.5 (`L = A₅`, `Hᵢ = A₆`) gives the hexagon, Example 8.7 every parachute of two chains, both core-free (the tuple is faithful).  The groups are enormous (the hexagon's socle is `A₅^2160`), but existence is what Theorem 3.6 consumes: **any two classes cf-IE via the three-element chain share a member**, and more generally any two classes enforced by canopies of the form "dual of such an interval plus a top" do.  This is `chain₃-pair-intersects` of Entry 11, and it kills the RP-3 sweep's first target `P(3,3)` as a non-representability shortcut.

**The hexagon by search**.  Prompted by Proposition 1, the tables-of-marks scan (skill `hunting-lattice-intervals-in-tomlib`, a minute over the 414 tables) found the hexagon as a marks-exact upper interval in sixteen tables, all confirmed by `IntermediateSubgroups` where checked: the smallest carrier is `U₄(2)` (order 25920, `H ≅ (C₆ × C₂) : C₂` of order 24, index 1080, chains of orders 96 < 960 and 216 < 648), then `U₃(7)` (index 235984, `H ≅ S₄`), and the **alternating** group `A₁₁` over `H = 11 : 5`, whose chains are the two `PSL(2 , 11)` and the two `M₁₁` above the normalizer of a Sylow 11-subgroup.  The pipeline verdicts are `scripts/gap/flrp/out/hexagon_*.search.json`.  Two things follow.  The `A₁₁` interval refutes enforcement of `𝒢₁` by the hexagon (Entry 11, the second negative entry after Entry 8), and it settles the reading of Aschbacher's 2012 sentence quoted in § 3: a D-lattice does occur in an alternating group, so the sentence is about the class `D(m₁ , … , mₜ)`, as the zbMATH review of the Aschbacher–Shareshian paper states.  And the minimal carrier of the hexagon as a group interval now lies between the RP-3 sweep's bound (no group of order at most 300) and 25920; every ambiguous table below that order resolved negative.

**What the three Elsevier texts add**.  Aschbacher–Shareshian's Theorem 1.1 is exactly the statement Entry 12 imports, with the hypotheses `t > 1` and `m₁ ≥ ⋯ ≥ mₜ ≥ 3`; their proof splits on whether `H` is intransitive, imprimitive, or primitive on the natural set, and the primitive case of non-prime degree is dispatched by citing Theorem D of Aschbacher's *Overgroups of primitive groups II*.  Their Lemma 6.2, that every member of a `DΔ`-interval in any finite group is self-normalizing, is the `DΔ` counterpart of RP-1's `normal-in-proper-trivial` stated without core-freeness; for parachutes the core-free reduction already gives the stronger statement, so there is nothing to add.  The 2013 paper's Theorem 1 concerns `DΔ`-lattices and Boolean lattices in groups of Lie type and does not touch parachutes.

**Theorem D, in parachute terms**.  Aschbacher calls a lattice an *I-lattice* when every coatom has another coatom meeting it in the bottom; every disconnected lattice is one, so every parachute with at least two canopies is one.  Theorem D (§ 3) says that for `H` primitive in an alternating or symmetric group `G` of non-prime degree with `O_G(H)′′` an I-lattice, the lattice is `M₂`, `T_{2,2}`, `T_{1,2,2}`, `T_{2,1,1,1}`, `M_{1,3}`, `T₁ ∗ H₇`, or `T₋₁Γ(k)`, and that each of these occurs.  Here `O_G(H)′′` is the set of overgroups of `H` not containing `F*(G)` with `G` adjoined, which is `O_G(H)` itself when `G` is alternating or when `G` is symmetric and `H` is not inside the alternating group; `Tₘ` is the chain with `m + 2` elements, `T_{m₁,…,mᵣ}` glues the proper parts of the `Tₘᵢ` side by side, so it is the parachute of chains with `mᵢ + 1` elements; `Γ(k)` is the divisor lattice of `k`, and `T₋₁Γ(k)` is the parachute of a two-element chain and `Γ(k)` with a new top (Aschbacher 2008's Example 8.6 family); `M_{1,3}` and `T₁ ∗ H₇` have a component with no least element and are not parachutes.  Reading the list: **the only parachutes with two big canopies that arise from a primitive subgroup of non-prime degree in an alternating or symmetric group are the hexagon `T_{2,2}` and `P(3,3,2) = T_{1,2,2}`**, and both do arise; every other such parachute (`P(3,4)`, `P(3,2×2)`, `P(3,3,3)`, `P(4,4)`, and so on) is excluded from that case outright.  The `A₁₁` hexagon has prime degree and falls outside the theorem's hypothesis, as it must, and its two seven-element cousins found by the tables-of-marks scan sit in `PΓL(2,8)`, `PSL(2,25)`, and `U₄(3).2₁`, not in alternating or symmetric groups.  What this leaves open for an Aschbacher–Shareshian-style theorem about parachutes in `Aₙ`/`Sₙ` is the imprimitive and intransitive cases and the prime degrees, for which their Section 5 diamond lemma (Theorem 5.2, the structure of `H = G₁ ∩ G₂` when `G₁` is the stabilizer of a regular partition and `M(H) = {G₁ , G₂}` with `H` maximal in both) is the tool; that is a concrete question for the F10 direction, recorded in § 6.

**What an F10 entry would need**.  The RP-3 note's surviving direction asks for enforcement theorems at the level of the monolith and the action on it.  Proposition 2 supplies the *form* of such an entry (transitivity on components, the interval inside the monolith) but not a separating invariant, and the reduction theorems that would supply one do not reach parachutes.  So the Aschbacher engagement does not reopen the candidate table; it sharpens the statement of what would.  (M6-27 then obtained one such theorem, Theorem A of § 4.13, homogeneity of the coatom type; it does not separate either.)

### 4.13  The parachute analog of Theorem 3 (M6-27)

Recorded in full in `docs/notes/flrp-parachute-theorem3.md`; the summary.  For a **coatomistic** parachute `Λ` with two big canopies (every canopy coatomistic including its bottom; `P(2×2 , 2×2)` is the least) and a core-free finite representation `(H , G)` with monolith `D`, one of four alternatives holds: `D` is simple, so `G` is almost simple; or `H` is a complement to `D` and `[H , G]` is one of Aschbacher's signalizer lattices `Λ(τ)`; or `Λ ≅ O_Ḡ(H̄)` for the smaller almost simple group `Ḡ = Aut_G(L)`; or the dual of `Λ` is an interval in a group of order below `|G|`.  Over Aschbacher's dual-closed minimality the first two remain, which is the conclusion of his Theorem 3; over minimality among representations of `Λ` alone the fourth remains too, and that is the one place the analog is weaker than the original, since the dual of a parachute is never coatomistic and the argument cannot be rerun on the dual side.  The proof is his Section 6 with (6.5) fed by the coatomistic condition and (6.6)(3) fed by two coatoms of one canopy; it uses the classification of finite simple groups through his (5.3), as the original does.  On the way it proves, with no minimality at all, that in every core-free representation of a parachute two of whose canopies have two coatoms, with non-simple monolith, the maximal overgroups of `H` are all of product type or all of diagonal type (Theorem A); both types are realized, product type by `Ḡ ≀ C₂` over `H̄ ≀ C₂` for any almost simple representation `[H̄ , Ḡ]`, diagonal type by the Kurzweil wreaths, so the type is not an F10 invariant.  Formal: `FLRP.Reductions.Coatomistic` (Entry 13, the theorem imported as `ParachuteTheorem3`, the two corollaries derived) on the coatom vocabulary of `Classical.Structures.Lattice.Disconnected`.  Vacuity datum: **unknown**.  No coatomistic parachute with two big canopies is known to be a congruence lattice.  `P(2×2 , 2×2)`, being a simple lattice whose coatoms meet to `0` (computed from its tables), has, if it has a representation at all, a minimal one whose operations are permutations (Pálfy–Pudlák, McKenzie) generating a transitive group (DeMeo–Freese's theorem on intransitive actions, since the only partition-lattice filter of the parachute is the two-element one over a coatom, below which lies an indecomposable chain), so it is a congruence lattice if and only if it is a group interval, and the same holds for every coatomistic parachute whose canopies have no nontrivial congruence keeping the top alone (`docs/notes/flrp-parachute-theorem3.md` § 7.1, Theorem E).  None is known: `P(2×2 , 2×2)` is in none of TomLib's 414 tables, in no group of order at most 300, in no `Eq(n)` for `n ≤ 7`, and over no point stabilizer of a transitive group of composite degree at most 22.  For part of 2026-09-28 this paragraph said the entry was non-vacuous, on Kjos-Hanssen's classification of `P(2×2 , 2×2)` as a *parallel sum*: his note describes the parallel sum as identifying the tops and the bottoms of two lattices, and a parachute identifies the tops of its canopies.  Snow's parallel sum (Lemmas 3.9 and 3.10, read in the text the same day) adjoins a new top and bottom instead, and closure under the glued operation would make every `Mₙ` representable; the correction, and the census of the eight-element lattices it prompted (61 of the note's 168 "formal" lattices are in the same position, fourteen of them simple), is `docs/notes/flrp-lattices8-census.md`.  The hexagon, by contrast, *is* a Snow sum (`2 ∓ 2`) and the congruence lattice of a six-element algebra (`FLRP.Certificates.Parachute.HexagonEq6`) while its least known group carrier has order 25920.  The signalizer alternative goes one step further through the 2009 paper (its Corollary 4.11 needs only the coatomistic half): `F*(H)` is a transitive product of simple groups; the descent of its Section 5 to `Aut_H(E)` does not transfer (`docs/notes/flrp-parachute-theorem3.md` § 4.1).

### 4.14  Lucchini's dichotomy for `Mₙ` (M6-27)

The `Mₙ` literature is a reduction program older than Aschbacher's and the model for it, and its base theorem is now read in the primary text: Lucchini [1994] proves that for `n − 1` not a prime power and `n` large enough ("for example `n ≥ 50`") a group of least order with an `Mₙ` interval over `H` is almost simple, or `H` meets the socle trivially, or `n` is in one of his two representable families.  Entry 14 imports it as `LucchiniDichotomy` of `FLRP.Reductions.Lucchini`, over Entry 13's `Minimal` and `MonolithSimple`, with the monolith as an argument (Köhler's Entry 6 supplies it; the composition is derived) and the two families stated over the standard library's primes.  Baddeley–Lucchini [1997], read as well (§ 3), take the second alternative apart into tuples over finite simple groups; that tree is recorded in prose in `docs/notes/flrp-m6-27-paths.md` § 4, since its vocabulary is beyond the library.  Two things to know.  First, the reduction of both papers is for `n` beyond about `50`, and `M₁₆`, the smallest `Mₙ` not known to be representable, lies below: nothing in them excludes a non-simple socle meeting `H` nontrivially at `n = 16`.  Second, Lucchini's first reduction (1.1 through 1.8) contains the `Mₙ` prototypes of Aschbacher's (4.12)(7), (4.13), and (6.5), and Baddeley–Lucchini's "key step" (the socle of the complement is unique minimal normal and nonabelian) is what § 4.1 of the theorem note now gives for coatomistic parachutes.

## 5.  Entries considered and rejected

The failure mode of this phase is a plausible-sounding theorem statement that no paper actually proves.  These were considered and left out; a vague entry is worse than no entry.

+  **Baddeley–Lucchini 1997** (*On representing finite lattices as intervals in subgroup lattices of finite groups*, J. Algebra 196, 1–100).  *Resolved by M6-27* (§ 4.14): the text was supplied and read.  The min-IE disjunction the first pass expected is the base theorem of Lucchini 1994, now Entry 14; the 1997 paper's own results are a tree of tuple conditions over finite simple groups, recorded in prose in `docs/notes/flrp-m6-27-paths.md` § 4 and not stated in Agda, and its reduction begins above `n = 50`, so it does not reach `M₁₆`.
+  **Baddeley 1998** (*A new approach to the finite lattice representation problem*).  Same situation, wider target class.
+  **Börner 1999** (*A remark on the finite lattice representation problem*, Contributions to General Algebra 11).  Conference volume, not online, nothing located to verify.  Rejected.
+  **Aschbacher's `D∆` and signalizer theorems** (2008, 2009, 2012, 2013).  *Resolved by M6-21* (§ 4.12), with the primary texts of 2008, 2009, and 2012 read in full or in the relevant parts.  What could be stated was stated (Entries 10 and 11); what resisted is now precise rather than vague.  The reduction theorems, Theorem 3 of 2008 and Theorem 2 of 2009, are stated for CD- and `DΔ`-lattices, and **no parachute with a big canopy is one**: every canopy of a parachute has a least element, its atom, so the parachute is never atomistic.  But only the coatomistic half of the C condition is used in the proof of Theorem 3, and M6-27 found that both C-dependent steps go through for coatomistic parachutes ((6.6)(3) needs two coatoms of one component meeting *above* `H`, which the first reading, from a text with its `≠` signs dropped, had inverted; § 2).  The parachute analog is Entry 13, § 4.13, proved rather than transcribed, with a caveat about the dual.  The signalizer-lattice apparatus (`Λ(τ)`, the posets `W`, `P`, the lower signalizer lattices `Ξ(τ)`) is consequently not needed for any parachute statement and was not formalized.  A second, smaller mismatch: Aschbacher's minimality is over representations of a lattice or its dual, and `MinimallyIE` is not.
+  **Aschbacher–Shareshian 2009**.  *Resolved as Entry 12*, from secondary sources (§ 3): the theorem excludes the `DΔ(m₁ , … , mₜ)`-lattices from the alternating and symmetric groups.  It was hoped to be a second source for Entry 5; it is not, because neither `M₆` nor any parachute is a `DΔ`-lattice, and the wider reading of Aschbacher's 2012 summary ("not a D-lattice") is refuted by the hexagon interval in `A₁₁` (Entry 11).  The primary text was read later the same day (§ 3) and confirms the statement.
+  **Lucchini 1994's representable families** (`M_{q+2}`, `M_{(qᵗ+1)/(q+1)+1}`) and **Feit's `M₁₁`**.  Out of scope rather than vague: these are *representability* facts, which belong to the certificate and closure track (WP-6, the small-lattice census of issue #485), not to the enforcement catalog.  They would, however, supply vacuity data for further `Mₙ` entries.
+  **Burness–Liebeck–Shalev 2017** (special primes).  About generation of second maximal subgroups; not an enforcement statement.  Out of scope, retained on the roadmap's watchlist.

## 7.  Reading order

`FLRP.Reductions` opens with the vocabulary (vacuity, Lemma 3.1, exclusion, min-IE, `Mₙ`), then the three group classes, then the entries in the order of the table above.  A reader who wants the mathematics rather than the catalog should start at `Classical.Structures.Group.MinimalNormal` (small, self-contained), then the `Parachutes` module of `FLRP.Reductions` (where RP-1's Lemma 3.7 becomes three entries), then the `Compose` submodule.  For the Aschbacher material read `Classical.Structures.Lattice.Disconnected` (the classes, `D→A`, and the coatom vocabulary) before § 4.12 of this note and Entries 10 through 12, then § 4.13 with `FLRP.Reductions.Coatomistic` and `docs/notes/flrp-parachute-theorem3.md` for Entry 13, and § 4.14 with `FLRP.Reductions.Lucchini` for Entry 14.  The framework is `docs/notes/flrp-rp1-parachutes.md`; the program is `docs/notes/flrp-research-roadmap.md` § 4.
