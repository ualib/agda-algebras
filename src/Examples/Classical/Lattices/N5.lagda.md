### Worked example: the pentagon `N₅`

This is the [Examples.Classical.Lattices.N5][] module of the [Agda Universal Algebra Library][].

The pentagon `N₅` has a bottom `⊥`, a top `⊤`, a two-element chain `a < c`
between them, and an element `b` incomparable to both: `⊥ < a < c < ⊤` and
`⊥ < b < ⊤`.  It is the smallest lattice that is not modular.  Unlike the
diamond of [Examples.Classical.Lattices.M3][], it satisfies all three conditions
of [Classical.Properties.Lattice.Semidistributive][]: both semidistributive laws
and Whitman's condition (W).  By Nation's theorem ([Freese, Ježek, and Nation
(1995)][], Theorem 5.55), a finite lattice with these three properties is a
sublattice of a free lattice, so `N₅` is one.  For `N₅` this is also elementary:
`N₅` is generated by `a`, `b`, and `c`, so `FL(3)` maps onto it, and `FL(3)` is
therefore not modular; by Dedekind's criterion a lattice that is not modular has
a sublattice isomorphic to `N₅`.

As for `M₃`, the lattice is given by its tables through
[Classical.Structures.Lattice.Table][], and the three verdicts are the values of
the decision procedures, extracted by `from-yes`.

#### The tables

The carrier is `Fin 5`, with `⊥`, `a`, `b`, `c`, `⊤` numbered `0` to `4`.

| `∧` | ⊥ | a | b | c | ⊤ |   | `∨` | ⊥ | a | b | c | ⊤ |
|-----|---|---|---|---|---|---|-----|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⊥   | ⊥ | ⊥ | ⊥ | ⊥ | ⊥ |   | ⊥   | ⊥ | a | b | c | ⊤ |
| a   | ⊥ | a | ⊥ | a | a |   | a   | a | a | ⊤ | c | ⊤ |
| b   | ⊥ | ⊥ | b | ⊥ | b |   | b   | b | ⊤ | b | ⊤ | ⊤ |
| c   | ⊥ | a | ⊥ | c | c |   | c   | c | c | ⊤ | c | ⊤ |
| ⊤   | ⊥ | a | b | c | ⊤ |   | ⊤   | ⊤ | ⊤ | ⊤ | ⊤ | ⊤ |

```agda
infixl 7 _∧_ _∨_

⊥ a b c ⊤ : Fin 5
⊥ = 0F
a = 1F
b = 2F
c = 3F
⊤ = 4F

∧-table : Table 5
∧-table = (0F ∷ 0F ∷ 0F ∷ 0F ∷ 0F ∷ [])
        ∷ (0F ∷ 1F ∷ 0F ∷ 1F ∷ 1F ∷ [])
        ∷ (0F ∷ 0F ∷ 2F ∷ 0F ∷ 2F ∷ [])
        ∷ (0F ∷ 1F ∷ 0F ∷ 3F ∷ 3F ∷ [])
        ∷ (0F ∷ 1F ∷ 2F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ [])
        ∷ []

∨-table : Table 5
∨-table = (0F ∷ 1F ∷ 2F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ [])
        ∷ (1F ∷ 1F ∷ 4F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ [])
        ∷ (2F ∷ 4F ∷ 2F ∷ 4F ∷ 4F ∷ [])
        ∷ (3F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ [])
        ∷ (4F ∷ 4F ∷ 4F ∷ 4F ∷ 4F ∷ [])
        ∷ []

_∧_ _∨_ : Fin 5 → Fin 5 → Fin 5
_∧_ = ⟦ ∧-table ⟧
_∨_ = ⟦ ∨-table ⟧
```

#### The lattice

As for `M₃`, the laws are decided (`N₅-laws`{.AgdaFunction}), and the lattice
`N₅`{.AgdaFunction} and its enumeration `N₅-finite`{.AgdaFunction} are built
from them.

```agda
N₅-laws : LatticeTableLaws _∧_ _∨_
N₅-laws = from-yes (latticeTableLaws? _∧_ _∨_)

N₅ : Lattice 0ℓ 0ℓ
N₅ = tableLattice _∧_ _∨_ N₅-laws

N₅-finite : FiniteAlgebra (proj₁ N₅)
N₅-finite = tableLattice-FiniteAlgebra _∧_ _∨_ N₅-laws
```

#### The verdicts

All three procedures answer yes.

```agda
N₅-joinSemidistributive : IsJoinSemidistributive N₅
N₅-joinSemidistributive = from-yes (isJoinSemidistributive? N₅ N₅-finite)

N₅-meetSemidistributive : IsMeetSemidistributive N₅
N₅-meetSemidistributive = from-yes (isMeetSemidistributive? N₅ N₅-finite)

N₅-satisfiesW : SatisfiesW N₅
N₅-satisfiesW = from-yes (satisfiesW? N₅ N₅-finite)
```
