### Substitutions and the homomorphisms they induce

This is the [Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Substitution][] module of the [Agda Universal Algebra Library][].

A *substitution* `σ` from generators `X` to generators `Y` assigns a lattice term
over `Y` to each generator in `X`.  Applying it to a term `t` over `X` replaces
each generator `x` in `t` by `σ x`, and the result `t [ σ ]` is a term over `Y`.
Since `FL(Y)` is the set of terms over `Y`, a substitution is the same thing as a
map from `X` to `FL(Y)`, and by the universal property of
[Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Universal][] it extends to a unique
homomorphism `FL(X) → FL(Y)`; this module shows that the extension *is*
substitution.  Every homomorphism `FL(X) → FL(Y)` arises this way, from its
values on the generators, and the endomorphisms of `FL(X)` are the substitutions
from `X` to `X`.

Two open problems about free lattices are statements about such maps.
McNulty's free-lattice Nullstellensatz problem asks which equations hold under
every *solution* of a set of equations in `FL(n)`, a solution being an `n`-tuple
of elements of `FL(n)`, that is, an endomorphism; and Dean's problem asks about
chains of sublattices of a free lattice generated by finite sets, that is, about
images of `FL(n)` under substitutions.  [Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Closure][],
[Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Generation][], and
[Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Embedding][] build the tools for those
problems on the facts proved here: substitution is monotone, it commutes with
duality and with joins of lists, the value of a substituted term in any lattice
is the value of the original term under the substituted values (the evaluation
lemma), and an injective substitution reflects Whitman's order.

The argument order of `Sub X Y`{.AgdaFunction} follows the arrow of the induced
map, `FL(X) → FL(Y)`.  The generic substitutions of [Setoid.Terms.Basic][] list
their arguments the other way round; the two never meet, since the free-lattice
development works over `LatTerm`{.AgdaDatatype} rather than `Term`{.AgdaDatatype}.

#### Substitutions

`Sub X Y`{.AgdaFunction} is the type of substitutions from `X` to `Y`, and
`t [ σ ]`{.AgdaFunction} applies `σ` to `t` by structural recursion: a generator
is replaced by its image, and the formal operations are kept.

```agda
Sub : Type χ → Type υ → Type (χ ⊔ υ)
Sub X Y = X → LatTerm Y

infixl 30 _[_]

_[_] : LatTerm X → Sub X Y → LatTerm Y
ℊ x      [ σ ] = σ x
(s ∧̇ t)  [ σ ] = s [ σ ] ∧̇ t [ σ ]
(s ∨̇ t)  [ σ ] = s [ σ ] ∨̇ t [ σ ]
```

The generator embedding `ℊ`{.AgdaInductiveConstructor} is the identity
substitution (`[]-ℊ`{.AgdaFunction}), and substitutions compose:
`σ ⨟ τ`{.AgdaFunction} applies `σ` and then `τ`, and applying the composite is
applying the two in turn (`[]-⨟`{.AgdaFunction}).  Both equations hold up to
`_≡_`{.AgdaDatatype}, by induction on the term.

```agda
[]-ℊ : (t : LatTerm X) → t [ ℊ ] ≡ t
[]-ℊ (ℊ x)    = refl
[]-ℊ (s ∧̇ t)  = cong₂ _∧̇_ ([]-ℊ s) ([]-ℊ t)
[]-ℊ (s ∨̇ t)  = cong₂ _∨̇_ ([]-ℊ s) ([]-ℊ t)

infixr 9 _⨟_

_⨟_ : Sub X Y → Sub Y Z → Sub X Z
(σ ⨟ τ) x = σ x [ τ ]

[]-⨟ : (t : LatTerm X) (σ : Sub X Y) (τ : Sub Y Z) → t [ σ ⨟ τ ] ≡ t [ σ ] [ τ ]
[]-⨟ (ℊ x)    σ τ = refl
[]-⨟ (s ∧̇ t)  σ τ = cong₂ _∧̇_ ([]-⨟ s σ τ) ([]-⨟ t σ τ)
[]-⨟ (s ∨̇ t)  σ τ = cong₂ _∨̇_ ([]-⨟ s σ τ) ([]-⨟ t σ τ)
```

#### Substitution preserves Whitman's order

A derivation of `s ≤ʷ t` by Whitman's rules is carried, rule by rule, to a
derivation of `s [ σ ] ≤ʷ t [ σ ]` (`[]-mono`{.AgdaFunction}).  Substitution
keeps the formal operations, so rules 2, 3, and 6 survive with their own
constructors; rule 1 becomes reflexivity, since both sides become `σ x`; and
rules 4 and 5, whose generator may become any term, become the weakening lemmas
of [Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Whitman][] that extend them to every
shape.  Substitution therefore respects equality in the free lattice
(`[]-cong`{.AgdaFunction}), which is what makes it a well-defined map out of
`FL(X)`.  (By soundness the same follows from evaluating in `FL(Y)`; the direct
induction keeps the proof syntactic, as the rest of the development is.)

```agda
module _ (σ : Sub X Y) where
  []-mono : s ≤ʷ t → s [ σ ] ≤ʷ t [ σ ]
  []-mono (∨≤ p q)    = ∨≤ ([]-mono p) ([]-mono q)
  []-mono (ℊ≤∧ p q)   = ≤∧ ([]-mono p) ([]-mono q)
  []-mono (∧≤∧ p q)   = ∧≤∧ ([]-mono p) ([]-mono q)
  []-mono (ℊ≤ℊ refl)  = ≤ʷ-refl
  []-mono (ℊ≤∨ˡ p)    = ≤∨ˡ ([]-mono p)
  []-mono (ℊ≤∨ʳ p)    = ≤∨ʳ ([]-mono p)
  []-mono (∧ˡ≤ℊ p)    = ∧ˡ≤ ([]-mono p)
  []-mono (∧ʳ≤ℊ p)    = ∧ʳ≤ ([]-mono p)
  []-mono (∧ˡ≤∨ p)    = ∧ˡ≤∨ ([]-mono p)
  []-mono (∧ʳ≤∨ p)    = ∧ʳ≤∨ ([]-mono p)
  []-mono (∧≤∨ˡ p)    = ∧≤∨ˡ ([]-mono p)
  []-mono (∧≤∨ʳ p)    = ∧≤∨ʳ ([]-mono p)

  []-cong : s ≈ʷ t → s [ σ ] ≈ʷ t [ σ ]
  []-cong (p , q) = []-mono p , []-mono q
```

Substitution is also a congruence in the substitution: substitutions whose
values are pointwise equal in `FL(Y)` give equal results
(`[]-congʳ`{.AgdaFunction}), by induction on the term with the congruence of
the formal operations.

```agda
[]-congʳ : (t : LatTerm X) {σ τ : Sub X Y} → (∀ x → σ x ≈ʷ τ x) → t [ σ ] ≈ʷ t [ τ ]
[]-congʳ (ℊ x)    e = e x
[]-congʳ (s ∧̇ t)  e = ∧̇-cong ([]-congʳ s e) ([]-congʳ t e)
[]-congʳ (s ∨̇ t)  e = ∨̇-cong ([]-congʳ s e) ([]-congʳ t e)
```

#### Joins, meets, and duals

Substitution commutes with the join and the meet of a nonempty list
(`⋁⁺-[]`{.AgdaFunction}, `⋀⁺-[]`{.AgdaFunction}), and with the duality of
[Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Duality][]: the dual of a substituted term is
the dual term under the dual substitution (`dual-[]`{.AgdaFunction}).  The
second fact lets a theorem about joins of images be read for meets of images,
as the free generating sets of [Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Generation][]
do.

```agda
⋁⁺-[] : (σ : Sub X Y) (u : LatTerm X) (us : List (LatTerm X))
      → ⋁⁺ u us [ σ ] ≡ ⋁⁺ (u [ σ ]) (map (_[ σ ]) us)
⋁⁺-[] σ u []        = refl
⋁⁺-[] σ u (w ∷ ws)  = cong (u [ σ ] ∨̇_) (⋁⁺-[] σ w ws)

⋀⁺-[] : (σ : Sub X Y) (u : LatTerm X) (us : List (LatTerm X))
      → ⋀⁺ u us [ σ ] ≡ ⋀⁺ (u [ σ ]) (map (_[ σ ]) us)
⋀⁺-[] σ u []        = refl
⋀⁺-[] σ u (w ∷ ws)  = cong (u [ σ ] ∧̇_) (⋀⁺-[] σ w ws)

dual-[] : (t : LatTerm X) (σ : Sub X Y) → dual (t [ σ ]) ≡ dual t [ dual ∘ σ ]
dual-[] (ℊ x)    σ = refl
dual-[] (s ∧̇ t)  σ = cong₂ _∨̇_ (dual-[] s σ) (dual-[] t σ)
dual-[] (s ∨̇ t)  σ = cong₂ _∧̇_ (dual-[] s σ) (dual-[] t σ)
```

#### The evaluation lemma

In any lattice `𝑳`{.AgdaBound}, the value of a substituted term `t [ σ ]` under an
assignment `η`{.AgdaBound} of the generators `Y` is the value of `t` under the
assignment that sends each `x` to the value of `σ x` under `η`{.AgdaBound}
(`⟦[]⟧`{.AgdaFunction}).  This is the lattice-term form of the
`substitution`{.AgdaFunction} lemma of [Setoid.Terms.Basic][].  It holds up to
`_≡_`{.AgdaDatatype}, not merely up to the equality of `𝑳`{.AgdaBound}, because
evaluation sends each formal operation to the operation of `𝑳`{.AgdaBound}
clause by clause.

```agda
module _ (𝑳 : Lattice α ρ) where
  open Evaluation 𝑳  using ( ⟦_⟧ )
  open Lattice-Op 𝑳  using ( _∧_ ; _∨_ )

  ⟦[]⟧ : (t : LatTerm X) (σ : Sub X Y) (η : Y → 𝕌[ proj₁ 𝑳 ])
       → ⟦ t [ σ ] ⟧ η ≡ ⟦ t ⟧ (λ x → ⟦ σ x ⟧ η)
  ⟦[]⟧ (ℊ x)    σ η = refl
  ⟦[]⟧ (s ∧̇ t)  σ η = cong₂ _∧_ (⟦[]⟧ s σ η) (⟦[]⟧ t σ η)
  ⟦[]⟧ (s ∨̇ t)  σ η = cong₂ _∨_ (⟦[]⟧ s σ η) (⟦[]⟧ t σ η)
```

In the free lattice `FL(Y)` itself, evaluating a term over `X` under the
assignment `σ` *is* substitution (`⟦⟧-[]`{.AgdaFunction}), because the operations
of `FL(Y)` are the formal ones.  This is the form in which
[Setoid.Varieties.SoundAndComplete][] states its `evaluation`{.AgdaFunction} lemma
for the relatively free algebra `𝔽[ X ]`{.AgdaFunction}, and the special case
`σ = ℊ` is `⟦⟧-ℊ`{.AgdaFunction} of [Classical.Structures.Lattice.Free.Universal][].

```agda
module _ (σ : Sub X Y) where
  open Evaluation (FL Y) using ( ⟦_⟧ )

  ⟦⟧-[] : (t : LatTerm X) → ⟦ t ⟧ σ ≡ t [ σ ]
  ⟦⟧-[] (ℊ x)    = refl
  ⟦⟧-[] (s ∧̇ t)  = cong₂ _∧̇_ (⟦⟧-[] s) (⟦⟧-[] t)
  ⟦⟧-[] (s ∨̇ t)  = cong₂ _∨̇_ (⟦⟧-[] s) (⟦⟧-[] t)
```

#### The induced homomorphism

Substitution by `σ` is a setoid map `FL(X) → FL(Y)`, by `[]-cong`{.AgdaFunction},
and a homomorphism (`subst-hom`{.AgdaFunction}): the meet of `FL(X)` applied to an
argument tuple is the formal meet of its two entries, and substitution sends a
formal meet to the formal meet of the substituted entries, so compatibility holds
by reflexivity, and likewise for joins.  By `⟦⟧-[]`{.AgdaFunction}, this is the
homomorphism `FL-extend (FL Y) σ`{.AgdaFunction} of the universal property, with
substitution as its underlying map rather than evaluation.  The endomorphisms of
`FL(X)` are the case `Y = X`.

```agda
module _ (σ : Sub X Y) where
  subst-hom : hom (proj₁ (FL X)) (proj₁ (FL Y))
  subst-hom = mkhom (proj₁ (FL X)) (proj₁ (FL Y)) h (λ {f} → compatible {f})
    where
    h : Func 𝔻[ proj₁ (FL X) ] 𝔻[ proj₁ (FL Y) ]
    h = record { to = _[ σ ] ; cong = []-cong σ }

    compatible : compatible-map (proj₁ (FL X)) (proj₁ (FL Y)) h
    compatible {∧-Op} = ≈ʷ-refl
    compatible {∨-Op} = ≈ʷ-refl
```

#### Every homomorphism is a substitution

A homomorphism `h`{.AgdaBound} from `FL(X)` to `FL(Y)` is determined by its
values on the generators, its `restriction`{.AgdaFunction}, which is a
substitution: `h`{.AgdaBound} agrees on every term with substitution by its
restriction (`hom-induced`{.AgdaFunction}).  This is the uniqueness half of the
universal property, `FL-extend-unique`{.AgdaFunction} at `𝑳 = FL Y`, read
through `⟦⟧-[]`{.AgdaFunction}.

```agda
module _ (h : hom (proj₁ (FL X)) (proj₁ (FL Y))) where
  restriction : Sub X Y
  restriction x = proj₁ h ⟨$⟩ ℊ x

  hom-induced : (t : LatTerm X) → proj₁ h ⟨$⟩ t ≈ʷ t [ restriction ]
  hom-induced t =
    subst (proj₁ h ⟨$⟩ t ≈ʷ_) (⟦⟧-[] restriction t)
          (FL-extend-unique (FL Y) restriction h (λ _ → ≈ʷ-refl) t)
```

#### Injective substitutions reflect the order

A lattice homomorphism that is injective reflects the order, because the order is
defined by the operations: if `s [ σ ] ≤ t [ σ ]`, then `(s ∧ t) [ σ ]` and
`s [ σ ]` are equal, so `s ∧ t` and `s` are, and `s ≤ t`
(`injective→reflects`{.AgdaFunction}).  Conversely a substitution that reflects
the order is injective (`reflects→injective`{.AgdaFunction}).  An injective
homomorphism `FL(X) → FL(Y)` is a lattice *embedding* in the sense of
[Freese, Ježek, and Nation (1995)][], Chapter IX, and these two lemmas let the
later modules use whichever form is convenient.

```agda
module _ (σ : Sub X Y) where
  injective→reflects : IsInjective (proj₁ (subst-hom σ)) → s [ σ ] ≤ʷ t [ σ ] → s ≤ʷ t
  injective→reflects {s = s} {t} inj p =
    ≤ʷ-trans (proj₂ (inj {s ∧̇ t} {s} (∧̇-lowerˡ , ≤∧ ≤ʷ-refl p))) ∧̇-lowerʳ

  reflects→injective : (∀ {s t : LatTerm X} → s [ σ ] ≤ʷ t [ σ ] → s ≤ʷ t)
                     → IsInjective (proj₁ (subst-hom σ))
  reflects→injective refl≤ (p , q) = refl≤ p , refl≤ q
```
